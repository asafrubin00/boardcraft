# BoardCraft — Business Plan

*Prepared June 2026. Companion to [ASSESSMENT.md](./ASSESSMENT.md), which contains the product audit and the P0/P1/P2 technical roadmap this plan sequences against. Written for a solo founder, bootstrapped, building with AI assistance on a constrained budget.*

---

## 1. Executive Summary

BoardCraft is a corporate governance strategy game that has earned an unprompted, memorable positioning from its target market: **"fantasy football for corporate governance."** Board effectiveness professionals — the hardest audience to impress and the easiest to sell through — have validated that it's valuable. The opportunity is to convert that enthusiasm into a licensing business serving governance training before the window closes.

The strategy in one paragraph: **harden the product (3 weeks), sell workshops to the warm network before building anything new (this autumn), let paying clients pull the platform features into existence (winter), and lock distribution partnerships before content-generation becomes a commodity (2027).** The defensible asset is not the scenarios — generative AI is rapidly making scenario *content* cheap to imitate. What compounds and defends is: credibility with the professional community, accreditation, distribution relationships with institutions, and the quality of the learning debrief. Speed matters most in locking those, not in shipping features.

Honest framing of scale: this is most plausibly a **£100k–£500k/year lifestyle-to-boutique business within 2–3 years** on the licensing model, with optionality to grow beyond that if a professional body partnership or consumer flywheel catches. It is not a venture-scale story unless deliberately repositioned, and it doesn't need to be one to be worth doing.

## 2. The Market

### 2.1 Why now

- **NED demand and turnover are structurally rising.** Governance codes (FRC 2024 Code, CSRD, expanding US disclosure) keep raising the bar; boards refresh faster; the pipeline of *aspiring* NEDs taking certificates has grown every year.
- **Director education is dated.** The dominant formats are lectures, case-study discussions, and PDF-heavy e-learning. Experiential simulation is the proven pedagogy (used everywhere in exec ed) but governance-specific simulations are rare, bespoke, facilitator-dependent, and expensive.
- **The window:** generative AI means a competent consultancy can now produce a passable governance scenario pack in weeks. BoardCraft's 18-month content head start erodes. The durable position is to be the *platform and brand* the profession already uses when imitators arrive.

### 2.2 Who buys (segments, in order of attack)

| Segment | What they buy | Why BoardCraft wins | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Board advisory / effectiveness consultancies** | A differentiated workshop tool for board awaydays and evaluations | Makes their facilitation memorable; they bill the client, BoardCraft is their edge | 2–3 design partners from the existing warm network |
| **Executive education & universities** | Course module for governance/strategy programmes (LSE is the obvious first pilot) | Side-by-side jurisdictions (UK/US/DE/charity) is unique teaching material | One term of classroom use + instructor guide |
| **Professional bodies** (IoD, CGI UK&I, NACD, GUBERNA…) | Member benefit, course component, CPD-accredited practice tool | Their members *are* the audience; accreditation converts play into CPD hours | Pilot data from segments 1–2 first; they move slowly |
| **Corporates (CoSec / L&D)** | Director induction & board-readiness for senior executives | Safe-to-fail practice before a first board seat | Bespoke scenario capability (P2 pipeline) |
| **Aspiring NEDs (consumer)** | Subscription self-study alongside NED diplomas | Cheap to serve once built; marketing flywheel | Defer until B2B is proving out |

### 2.3 Competition

Direct gamified governance simulation at this fidelity: effectively none at consumer-accessible price points. The real competition is (a) the status quo — case-study teaching and bespoke facilitated simulations run by big-firm board practices at £20k+/day, and (b) the *imminent* competition: AI-generated scenario content from any consultancy. BoardCraft's compliance engine with real code citations, four genuinely different regulatory regimes, and a working game economy is 12–18 months of head start; treat it as a depreciating asset and convert it into relationships and accreditation while it's fresh.

## 3. Product & Pricing Architecture

Three product tiers, built in this order because each funds and de-risks the next:

### Tier 1 — Facilitated Workshop License (sell first, exists ~now)
The founder (later: certified facilitators) runs a 2–3 hour session for a board, a cohort, or a class using the existing web product.
- **Price hypothesis:** £1,500–£3,000 per session direct; £500–£1,000/session license fee when a consultancy facilitates.
- **Gate:** ASSESSMENT P0 fixes only. No backend needed — the facilitator *is* the dashboard.
- **Purpose:** revenue this quarter, testimonials, and pricing discovery with near-zero build cost.

### Tier 2 — Institutional License (sell on pilot evidence, build winter 2026)
Annual site license for a business school, professional body, or training firm: cohort management, completion tracking, facilitator dashboard, the **Governance Debrief report** (ASSESSMENT item 9 — the artifact that justifies per-seat pricing).
- **Price hypothesis:** £5k–£20k/year depending on seats; CPD accreditation lifts willingness to pay.
- **Gate:** ASSESSMENT P1 (especially the debrief) + a minimal backend (accounts, cohorts). Build only after at least one buyer has verbally committed at a named price.

### Tier 3 — Bespoke Scenario Commissions (the high-margin product, 2027)
"Your company, your sector, your jurisdiction, as a BoardCraft scenario" — for director induction, board awaydays, regulator-driven training (banks, NHS trusts, housing associations are all under governance pressure with budget to spend).
- **Price hypothesis:** £10k–£30k per commissioned scenario plus annual hosting.
- **Gate:** ASSESSMENT P2 content pipeline, proven by building a fifth company through it. This is the gate between "founder demos a game" and "business sells scenarios" — do not sell Tier 3 before the pipeline exists; a hand-built bespoke scenario would consume months.

**Consumer subscription** (£10–15/month, aspiring NEDs) stays parked until B2B is repeatable — it needs accounts, payments, support, and marketing the solo founder can't spare yet. Keep one free company (Harwick) public as the perpetual demo and lead-magnet.

## 4. Go-to-Market: the 12-Month Game Plan

### Phase 0 — Harden (mid-June → mid-July 2026)
*Goal: nothing in the product embarrasses you in front of an expert.*
- Execute ASSESSMENT P0 (items 1–6): bespoke Vantage/Rheinfeld AGMs, risk-flag fix, mid-game persistence, code-vs-heuristic labeling, phantom event cleanup, working tests.
- Form the company (UK Ltd), confirm IP ownership is clean (see §7 — LSE affiliation), trademark search on "BoardCraft," basic terms-of-use refresh.
- Write the one-page facilitator guide + a 10-minute demo script.

### Phase 1 — Sell the workshop (July → October 2026)
*Goal: 5 paid sessions, 3 reference customers, real pricing data. Summer is dead for exec ed — use July/August to book September/October.*
- Go back to **every professional who praised it** with a specific ask: "I'm running paid pilot workshops in September at £X — will your firm take one?" Convert enthusiasm to calendar slots. Target: consultancies and one LSE course leader.
- Run the sessions personally. Collect: testimonials, objections, what they'd pay annually, and what's missing (expect: the debrief, cohort tracking — which validates P1 before building it).
- Build ASSESSMENT P1 in parallel (debrief report above all), prioritized by what pilot clients actually ask for.
- Marketing surface area, minimal: a landing page with the "fantasy football for corporate governance" quote and testimonials; 2–3 LinkedIn posts/month written from pilot observations (governance LinkedIn is small and high-trust — this is the channel); one conference talk or governance-podcast appearance if the network offers it.

### Phase 2 — License (November 2026 → February 2027)
*Goal: first 2–3 annual licenses signed; backend exists because a buyer demanded it.*
- Convert the best pilot relationships to Tier 2 annual licenses. Build the minimal backend (Supabase-class: accounts, cohorts, facilitator view) **only once a license is verbally committed.**
- Begin CPD accreditation (CPD UK certification is cheap and fast; an IoD/CGI conversation is slower — start it now, expect 6–12 months).
- Build the P2 content pipeline through the winter; prove it with company #5 built entirely through the new tooling.

### Phase 3 — Scale decision (March → June 2027)
*Goal: pick the growth lane with data.*
- If Tier 2 is repeating: hire/contract a facilitator or partner with a training firm (train-the-trainer + royalty per session — scales without the founder in every room).
- If bespoke demand surfaced in pilots: sell the first Tier 3 commission against the now-proven pipeline.
- If a professional body bites: prioritize that partnership above everything; it's the distribution moat.
- Revisit consumer subscription only here.

## 5. Financial Sketch (hypotheses to validate, not forecasts)

Bootstrapped; costs are near-zero (Vercel hosting, ~£300/year tooling incl. Claude, insurance ~£500, accountant ~£1k). Revenue scenarios for the first 12 months of selling (Sept 2026 → Aug 2027):

| Scenario | Workshops | Licenses | Bespoke | Year-1 revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Floor** (network politeness, no repeat) | 5 × £1.5k | 0 | 0 | ~£7.5k |
| **Base** (pilots convert) | 12 × £2k | 3 × £8k | 0 | ~£48k |
| **Strong** (a body or training firm partners) | 20 × £2k | 5 × £12k | 1 × £15k | ~£115k |

The floor scenario still buys the most important asset: certainty about whether this is a business, for ~3 months of evenings. Decision gate at **January 2027**: if zero licenses are signed or seriously negotiating by then, the licensing thesis is wrong — pivot to selling the content/IP to a training provider, or to the consumer route, rather than grinding.

## 6. Operating Model: Solo Founder + AI

This plan assumes the founder remains the only human, with Claude as the development team. Practical implications, learned from this project:

- **Documents in the repo are the institutional memory.** ASSESSMENT.md and this plan survive context windows; session summaries don't. Every significant build session should end by updating a `docs/` artifact (decision log, changelog) so the next session starts warm.
- **Budget tokens like build capacity.** One scoped feature per session, defined in writing first (the P0 list is already session-sized). Schedule heavy sessions right after usage resets, as done for this audit. If revenue lands, upgrading Claude Pro → Max is the cheapest "hire" available and should be the first reinvestment.
- **The founder's scarce hours go to selling, not building.** Anything Claude can do (code, drafts, scenario research) gets delegated; the founder owns relationships, facilitation, and editorial judgment on governance accuracy — the three things that can't be delegated.
- **Quality gate:** the working test suite (P0 item 6) plus a pre-demo smoke checklist protects every client session. A broken demo to a professional body costs more than any feature is worth.

## 7. Risks & Mitigations

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **AI commoditizes scenario content** — the stated fear, and it's real | High | Race to lock what doesn't commoditize: accreditation, institutional licenses, facilitator network, debrief/analytics quality, brand. 12-month urgency on Phases 1–2. |
| **IP ownership ambiguity** (built during LSE affiliation; folder lives in an LSE project directory) | High, cheap to resolve | Check LSE IP policy *before* first paid license. Student-created IP is typically student-owned unless built on a funded project — confirm in writing. |
| **Expert credibility failure** (a flagged bug or a wrong code citation in front of professionals) | High | P0 before any paid demo; annual "code currency" review each time FRC/GCGC/NYSE guidance updates; label game heuristics vs. real provisions (P0 item 4). |
| **Key-person dependency** (every workshop needs the founder) | Medium, grows with success | Facilitator guide from day one; train-the-trainer model in Phase 3; the debrief report automates part of the facilitator's value. |
| **Procurement friction** (accessibility, GDPR, security questionnaires) | Medium, arrives with Tier 2 | WCAG pass (P2 item 16) and a one-page data-protection statement before the first institutional contract; backend choice (UK/EU-hosted) made with GDPR in mind. |
| **Trademark conflict on "BoardCraft"** | Low-medium | £200 search now beats a forced rebrand after traction. |
| **Founder burnout / competing commitments** | Medium | The phase gates are also stop-loss points; the January 2027 decision gate is explicit permission to stop or pivot with the learning banked. |

## 9. Bottom Line

The product has already done the hardest thing a new product can do: made the right experts say "there's something really valuable here" without being asked. The plan above spends three weeks making the product worthy of their scrutiny, then deliberately sells the cheapest possible thing (the founder's time, with the game as the differentiator) before building anything speculative, and lets real buyers pull the platform into existence. The deadline that matters is not a feature date — it's locking professional-community credibility and one or two institutional relationships before AI-generated imitations arrive. Everything in this plan is sequenced to win that race on a solo founder's hours and a hobby-tier budget.
